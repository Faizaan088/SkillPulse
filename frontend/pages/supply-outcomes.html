Nominal vs. Effective Supply

Why headline enrollment statistics create catastrophic false confidence in industrial planning

CONVENTIONAL FLAW

The Nominal Supply Mirage

Standard government portals count admissions as talent ready for the workforce. If 340,000 candidates enroll in Industrial Training Institutes (ITIs) and vocational schemes, policymakers report "340,000 skilled technicians available."

Fatal Blindspots: Ignores 25% dropouts/exam failures, assumes candidates can relocate 300km with zero housing, ignores that 30% take gig/delivery work, and treats silence on phone surveys as successful employment.

SKILLPULSE REALITY

Effective Supply Formulation

SkillPulse calculates the true market availability by systematically penalizing nominal numbers across attrition, geographic immobility, sector diversion, and workplace survival.

Effective Supply = Enrolled × (1 - L_exam) × (1 - L_geo) × (1 - L_trade) × R_12m

Result: Out of 340,000 enrolled candidates, only 104,200 represent genuine, employable, trade-aligned candidates active in industry at 12 months.

Tracer Non-Response: Missing ≠ Failure

Resolving survey non-contact biases through multi-source administrative triangulation

The Survey Failure Trap

Conventional tracer studies rely on manual phone audits 6 months after graduation. Up to 45% of candidates do not answer or have switched numbers.

Typical government reports categorize all non-respondents as "unemployed" or "failed outcomes", severely penalizing high-performing rural training centres whose alumni simply migrated or replaced temporary pre-paid SIM cards.

Traditional Non-Response: Treated as 100% Unemployed (Fatal Bias)

Bayesian Non-Response Disentanglement

SkillPulse links multiple passive evidence streams to determine true alumni status without relying on intrusive voice calls:

  • EPFO & ESIC Micro-Matching: Monthly social security contributions confirm active formal employment.
  • Apprenticeship Portal (NAPS): Identifies students actively continuing in paid shopfloor training.
  • Higher Education Registers: Captures students transitioning into engineering diplomas.
  • District Chamber of Commerce Tenders: Identifies self-employed electrical/welding contractors.
