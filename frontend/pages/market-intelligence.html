Demand Signal Analysis

Strong

High demand observed across multiple validated sources.

Example: Persistent high volume of hiring for specific trades in manufacturing hubs.

Moderate

Stable demand reflecting standard replacement and growth.

Example: General administrative roles in established sectors.

Weak

Oversupply of skills or declining industry relevance.

Example: Obsolete technological skills being phased out.

Increasing

Upward trend identified in short-term data analysis.

Example: Rapidly adopting new compliance standards demanding new certs.

Declining

Downward trend observed indicating shrinking demand.

Example: Manual entry roles affected by automation.

Emerging

New skill clusters appearing with rising technical relevance.

Example: Early signals of green energy maintenance skills.

Handling Conflicting Sources

When job boards show low demand but industry consultations indicate high demand, SkillPulse uses Market Observability metrics to weigh offline reality heavier than digital absence, presenting a fused signal with appropriate confidence margins.

Market Observability

Key insight: Digital visibility ≠ actual demand

High Observability

Roles commonly posted on digital job boards and professional networks.

E.g., Software Engineering, Digital Marketing

Low Observability

Roles hired through local networks, direct recruitment, or physical notices.

E.g., Specialized manufacturing trades, regional logistics

Why this matters: Relying purely on digital signals creates biased training decisions that ignore real-world industrial needs.
